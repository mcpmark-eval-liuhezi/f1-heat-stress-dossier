# F1 Heat Stress Dossier — 2024 Hot-Climate Rounds

**Prepared for:** Sports-physiology group lab meeting (Friday)
**Thesis:** Formula 1 rounds run in genuinely hot climates are where driver thermoregulation is pushed hardest. This dossier assembles the factual backbone from live data sources: the five hot-climate rounds of the 2024 F1 calendar (the Middle Eastern races — Bahrain, Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Abu Dhabi — plus Singapore), the reference driver profile, and two peer-reviewed studies on heat stress, dehydration and performance in racing drivers.

## 3. Peer-Reviewed Literature

1. **Mollica et al.** — *No Impact of Heat Stress and Dehydration on Short Duration Simulated Motor-Racing Performance.*
   International Journal of Exercise Science, 2019. **PMID: 31523354**
   Assessed the effect of passive dehydration (1% and 3% body-mass loss, sauna) and heat stress on short simulated motor-racing performance. Lap and sector times were unchanged, though subjective ratings (alertness, concentration, comfort) deteriorated at both levels of dehydration. Authors flag that longer-duration tasks representative of real racing demands warrant further study.

2. **Brearley, Finn** — *Responses of motor-sport athletes to V8 supercar racing in hot conditions.*
   International Journal of Sports Physiology and Performance, 2007. **PMID: 19124905**
   Documents physiological responses of racing drivers performing demanding workloads in high cabin temperatures while wearing heavy heat-retardant clothing — directly relevant to the F1 cockpit environment.
